In this lecture we discussed…

Logistic regression predicts a probability for a yes/no outcome. A straight line cannot: it leaves [0, 1]. The logistic curve cannot leave it. The model is linear in the log-odds: log 1−𝑝 𝑝 = 𝑏0 + 𝑏1𝑋. 𝑒𝑏1 is an odds ratio; −𝑏0/𝑏1 is the 50% point. Coefficients are chosen by maximum likelihood, not least squares. A threshold turns a probability into a decision; the confusion matrix judges it. Accuracy needs a baseline, and generalisation needs held-out data. More than two categories: one equation per category against a reference, with the probabilities still adding to 1. Extrapolation, separation and sampling variability are the standing limits.
